# Eligibility Verification Workflow Failure Points

## Positioning

This workflow is positioned around process improvement, quality, patient experience, operational efficiency, and data-informed decision support.

## Failure Point 1: Coverage Not Verified for Date of Service

If insurance is checked too early, too late, or not checked for the exact date of service, inactive coverage may not be identified until after claim submission.

**Impact:** eligibility denial, delayed reimbursement, manual rework, patient billing confusion.

**Fix:** verify active coverage close to the appointment date and document the verification source.

## Failure Point 2: Payer or Plan Entered Incorrectly

Even when a patient has active insurance, the claim can still fail if the wrong payer, group number, plan type, or network status is selected.

**Impact:** wrong-payer rejection, unnecessary A/R delay, duplicate follow-up work.

**Fix:** match payer, plan, member ID, and group number before claim creation.

## Failure Point 3: Referral Requirement Missed

Some plans require referrals before specialty services. If the requirement is missed, the denial may not appear until after the visit.

**Impact:** referral denial, preventable rework, patient dissatisfaction.

**Fix:** add referral requirement review to the front-end verification checklist.

## Failure Point 4: Prior Authorization Requirement Not Flagged

Eligibility verification should connect to authorization awareness. If authorization requirements are missed, the visit may proceed without approval.

**Impact:** authorization denial, delayed payment, avoidable write-off risk.

**Fix:** flag authorization requirements before the appointment is financially cleared.

## Failure Point 5: Coordination of Benefits Not Confirmed

When primary and secondary payer order is incorrect, the claim may deny or require additional follow-up.

**Impact:** COB denial, claim delay, increased A/R work.

**Fix:** confirm payer order during verification and document the result.

## Analyst Summary

Eligibility verification is a quality-control checkpoint. It protects the revenue cycle before the claim is created and protects the patient from avoidable billing confusion after care is delivered.
